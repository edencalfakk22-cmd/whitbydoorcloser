A fire door only does its job if it closes and latches without anyone touching it. Ontario's Fire Code requires the closures in a fire separation to be maintained in operating condition, and the two findings we are called about most in Whitby buildings are a door that swings shut but stops just short of latching, and a door that has been wedged, chained or propped because staff found it inconvenient.

Propping is the one to stop first. A wedged stairwell, corridor or garbage-room door defeats the separation entirely, and it is one of the easiest things for an inspector to write up during a routine Whitby Fire and Emergency Services inspection. If traffic genuinely needs the door open — a moving day in a Downtown Whitby building, a delivery route through a Brooklin plaza's back corridor — the correct answer is an electromagnetic hold-open tied into the fire alarm so the door releases and closes on an alarm signal. Not a doorstop, not a fire extinguisher, not a folded piece of cardboard in the latch.

We work fire doors building-wide rather than one at a time: check every closer and self-closing device, confirm the door latches from any open position, correct latch speed and closer sizing, replace failed units, sort out the strike, latch and gasketing that are stopping the door seating properly, and hand back a written record of what was found and what was corrected for your fire safety plan file.

Fire Door Closers & Ontario Fire Code Compliance — your questions

Our fire door closes but does not latch. Is that a violation?

Treat it as one. A fire door has to close and latch on its own for the separation to hold, and a door resting against the strike is an open door as far as fire and smoke are concerned. It is a common finding in Whitby buildings and it usually corrects with latch-speed adjustment, proper closer sizing, or a strike and latch repair.

Can we prop the fire door open during deliveries or a move-in?

No. Wedging or chaining a fire door defeats the separation it exists to create and is one of the most frequently written-up items in multi-residential and commercial buildings across Durham. The compliant way to keep it open is a hold-open device released by the fire alarm, which we can fit to most fire doors.

Can you do a whole Whitby building at once?

Yes, and that is usually the most efficient way to handle it. We walk every fire separation, test each self-closing device, correct what we can on the spot, list what needs parts, and leave you a written record you can keep with your fire safety plan documentation for the next inspection.
