The oldest entrances in town hide their hardware, and not always deliberately. A Brock Street storefront that has been re-fronted twice since the 1950s can have a closer in the transom, a spring in the floor, and evidence of a third arrangement abandoned somewhere in between. The first job is working out what is actually in there.

Once it is identified, the winter story is the same everywhere. A floor-spring case sits at grade in the line people walk, so the cover-plate seal takes meltwater and road salt straight off boots every February, and downtown entrances that open directly onto the sidewalk get the worst of it. When the seal goes, the mechanism corrodes from the inside, and the symptoms follow in order: the leaf stops self-centring, the hold-open detent stops catching, oil weeps around the plate, and finally the door drops as the pivot inside wears away.

Getting at one means lifting the leaf off, opening the case, and re-shimming and re-aligning the door on its pivots on the way back — noticeably more work than a surface swap, and on a heavy heritage leaf more again. Where the cement case is sound, that is the whole job. Where it has corroded through, or where an old door needs re-hanging before any hardware will hold its adjustment, we would far rather tell you at the estimate stage than halfway through the afternoon.

FAQs

Floor Springs & Hidden Overhead Closers — your questions

Why did our floor spring fail after only a few winters?

Brine, mostly. The case sits at grade in the exact line people walk, so every February it takes meltwater and road salt straight off boots, and the seal at the cover plate is the only thing holding that out. Once the seal goes, the case corrodes from the inside. Downtown entrances right on the sidewalk get the worst of it.

The door has no closer on it anywhere. What is holding it shut?

Something hidden — either a unit concealed in the transom above the door, or a spring in the floor under the bottom pivot. On the older Brock and Dundas Street storefronts we find both, sometimes on the same frontage, because those entrances have been rebuilt over the decades. Working out which one you have is the first thing we do.

Our door is a heavy old timber leaf on a hidden closer. Is that fixable?

Generally yes, though it wants measuring rather than assuming. Heavy and oversized leaves on heritage frontages often carry hardware that was marginal when it was fitted, so the honest answer is sometimes that the door needs re-hanging, or the hardware upsizing, before anything will hold its adjustment. We tell you that at the estimate stage, not afterwards.
