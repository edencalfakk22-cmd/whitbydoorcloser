An exit device has one non-negotiable job: it releases the door in a single motion from the inside, with no key, no thumbturn and no special knowledge. Ontario's Building Code calls for panic hardware on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred, which covers a good deal of Whitby — the halls and arenas around Iroquois Park, community centres, places of worship, school gyms, and the restaurant and banquet rooms along Dundas and through Brooklin.

The failures are predictable. Vertical-rod devices on double doors drift out of adjustment and the top latch quietly stops engaging. Rim devices get dogged down permanently so the door never latches at all. Touch bars get bent by carts and pallet jacks in the industrial units, latches wear round and start slipping off the strike, and outside trim seizes after a winter of salt and grit blown in off the parking lot. On a fire-rated opening the device must not be dogged at all, and labelled fire exit hardware has to stay labelled — we do not put non-rated parts onto a rated assembly to save a trip.

We repair and adjust rim, mortise and surface vertical-rod devices, replace worn latches, dogging mechanisms and outside trim, sort out electric strikes and delayed-egress arrangements where they exist, and set the closer behind the device so the door latches cleanly instead of bouncing off the strike and standing open.

FAQs

Panic Bars & Exit Device Service — your questions

Which Whitby doors actually need panic hardware?

The Building Code requires it on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred — halls, arenas, restaurants, places of worship, gyms and similar. Plenty of other commercial and industrial exits carry exit devices by choice, because they are the most reliable way to guarantee one-motion egress from the inside.

Our exit door will not open from the inside. How fast can you come?

That is a life-safety emergency and we treat it as priority dispatch. Do not chain it, block it or route people past it in the meantime — station someone at the door and call us. We will get a technician to your Whitby address and get the opening working the way it has to.

Can we dog the panic bar down so the door stays unlatched during business hours?

On a non-rated door, mechanical dogging is a normal feature and we will set it up properly. On a fire-rated door, no — fire exit hardware must not be dogged, because the door has to latch to hold the separation. If you need that opening to stand open, a hold-open tied to the fire alarm is the right answer.
