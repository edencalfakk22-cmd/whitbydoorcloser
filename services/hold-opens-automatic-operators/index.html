There are two legitimate ways to keep a door standing open in a Whitby building, and neither of them is a wedge. The first is a hold-open device — a magnetic holder, or a closer with an integral electromagnetic hold-open — wired so it releases the door on a fire alarm signal. That is how a corridor or vestibule door in an apartment building, a long-term care home or a school can stay open for traffic all day and still close when it matters.

The second is a low-energy power operator: the push-plate operators on barrier-free entrances, washrooms and accessible suites. In a town with the Abilities Centre in it, nobody needs convincing that these matter. When an operator dies the door reverts to whatever the closer behind it is set at, and that is exactly when an entrance that was fine in October becomes unusable for someone in a chair in January — cold-thickened fluid and a spring that was wound up to beat a draft add up fast.

We install and service both: magnetic holders and alarm interfaces, closer-holder combination units, push plates, wireless actuators, operator arms and control boxes — and the closer behind the operator, so the door still behaves sensibly when the power is out or the unit is waiting on a part.

FAQs

Hold-Opens & Automatic Door Operators — your questions

Is a magnetic hold-open allowed on a fire door?

Yes, when it is tied into the fire alarm so the door releases and self-closes on an alarm signal. That is the compliant alternative to propping. What is never allowed is a doorstop, a wedge or a hook holding a fire door open with nothing to release it when the alarm sounds.

Our accessible push-plate operator stopped working. Can it be repaired?

Usually. Most failures are the actuator, the control board, a wiring fault or a worn operator arm rather than the whole unit. We diagnose on site and repair where parts are still available, replacing only when it genuinely makes sense to.

What happens to an automatic door in a power failure?

It falls back to the closer behind it, which is why that closer has to be set correctly rather than left slack because the operator normally does the work. We set the manual behaviour deliberately, so the door still closes and latches — and is still openable — when the operator is dead.
