Surface-mounted closers are the ones you can see above the door, and they are the bulk of our Whitby calls. Three failures cover almost all of them: the body has lost hydraulic fluid and the door either slams or has no control left at all; the arm, forearm or shoe bracket has been torn loose from a door that was shouldered or blown open past its limit; or the unit was undersized for the opening it was hung on and has been fighting a losing battle ever since.

A closer is a sealed hydraulic unit. There is no fill port, so a leaking body cannot be topped up no matter what a video tells you, and backing a valve out on a dry unit is how the last of the fluid ends up on the floor. What we can do is establish whether the closer is worth adjusting or has to come off, size the replacement properly for the door width, weight and exposure, and reinforce the mounting so the next gust across a Thickson Road parking field does not pull the arm out of the frame again.

Most of these finish in one visit. We carry common surface closers, arms, brackets, templates and sex bolts on the truck, so a slamming door in a Downtown Whitby storefront or a Brooklin plaza unit usually gets sorted the same day instead of being wedged open until a part arrives.

FAQs

Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

My door closer is leaking oil — can it be refilled?

No. Modern closers are sealed hydraulic bodies with no fill port, so once the fluid is out the unit has to be replaced. A leaking closer either slams the door hard enough to split a frame or stalls short of the latch, and on a fire door that second failure is a Fire Code problem, not just a nuisance.

The door slams. Can you just turn a screw and fix it?

Sometimes, yes. Sweep speed and latch speed are separate valves, and a door that travels fine then bangs at the end is often just a latch-speed adjustment. But if the body is dry or a valve has been backed out too far already, adjusting further makes it worse. We check the unit first and tell you honestly which one you have.

How long should a commercial door closer last in Whitby?

A properly sized unit on a normal commercial entrance lasts many years. What shortens it here is cycle count and weather — a plaza door taking salt, grit and freeze-thaw off the parking lot all winter, or a shop door in the industrial park cycling hundreds of times a shift, wears far faster than an interior office door. An undersized closer on a heavy exterior door fails early no matter what.
